# A. Treino de Força sem Complicação

## Reviewer profile requested

Qualified exercise / strength-and-conditioning professional competent to review general healthy-adult resistance-training education.

## Evidence anchor

American College of Sports Medicine, 2026 position stand:
*Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews.*

ACSM announcement:
https://acsm.org/science-spotlight-acsm-releases-new-position-stand-on-resistance-training/

WHO physical activity guidance:
https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity

## High-value claims that require explicit confirmation

### Consistency and complexity
The ebook frames consistency, appropriate effort and repeatable programming as more useful for most general users than unnecessarily advanced methods.

Reviewer:
- [ ] acceptable
- [ ] revise

### Strength loading
The ebook states that higher loads are especially relevant when the goal is maximizing strength, without telling beginners to test maximal loads.

Reviewer:
- [ ] acceptable
- [ ] revise

### Hypertrophy / weekly volume
The ebook describes approximately 10 weekly sets per muscle group as a practical optimization reference in many contexts — **not a universal minimum or prescription**.

Reviewer must confirm:
- [ ] source interpretation is acceptable;
- [ ] wording avoids a fixed universal target;
- [ ] novice/minimum-effective-volume context is sufficiently clear.

### Training to failure
Current framing:
- failure can be a tool;
- it is not mandatory for every set;
- fatigue/recovery cost matters.

Reviewer:
- [ ] acceptable
- [ ] revise

### Frequency
Current framing:
- train major muscle groups repeatedly across the week;
- frequency is partly a way to distribute volume and practice;
- no single split is presented as universally superior.

Reviewer:
- [ ] acceptable
- [ ] revise

### Range of motion and technique
Current framing deliberately avoids:
- one visually “perfect” technique for all bodies;
- forcing painful range;
- treating anatomical variation as error.

Reviewer:
- [ ] acceptable
- [ ] revise

### Pain / escalation
Current framing distinguishes ordinary effort/soreness from acute or concerning symptoms and states Coach X does not diagnose injuries.

Reviewer:
- [ ] stop/escalation language adequate;
- [ ] add/change escalation language.

## Program examples requiring review

The ebook includes example maps:
- 3-day full body;
- 4-day upper/lower.

These are labelled organization examples, not universal individualized prescriptions.

Reviewer should assess:
- balance of movement patterns;
- whether any example could be misread as mandatory;
- whether additional readiness language is needed.

## Final exercise-review checklist

- [ ] No maximal testing requirement for beginners.
- [ ] Volume language is contextual, not prescriptive.
- [ ] Failure language is appropriately conservative.
- [ ] Home-training examples do not encourage unsafe improvised supports.
- [ ] Pain/neurological/acute-symptom escalation is appropriate.
- [ ] General-adult scope is clear.
- [ ] Pregnancy, disease, active injury and clinical restrictions remain outside generic advice.
- [ ] Coach X boundaries are explicit.
- [ ] Approved version number/date/reviewer role recorded.

# B. Nutrição sem Ruído

## Reviewer profile requested

Qualified nutrition professional competent to review general healthy-adult nutrition education.

## Evidence anchors

WHO. *Healthy diet.* Updated 26 Jan 2026:
https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet

Ministério da Saúde. *Guia Alimentar para a População Brasileira*:
https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/composicao/saps/promocao-da-saude/guias-alimentares/guias-alimentares

USDA FoodData Central:
https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/

## Core framing to confirm

The ebook prioritizes:
- adequacy;
- balance;
- moderation;
- diversity;
- safe food;
- foods and meals before isolated nutrients;
- culture, budget, access and repeatability;
- no mandatory calorie counting.

Reviewer:
- [ ] acceptable
- [ ] revise

## Protein section

Current approach:
- names common protein sources;
- does not prescribe an individualized gram/kg target;
- explicitly says needs vary with body size, training, goal, age, energy intake and clinical context.

Reviewer:
- [ ] acceptable for general education;
- [ ] add population-specific caution;
- [ ] revise.

## Carbohydrate and fat sections

Current approach avoids:
- “good/bad” carbohydrate absolutism;
- universal low-fat advice;
- universal food exclusion.

Reviewer:
- [ ] acceptable
- [ ] revise

## Processing / Brazilian food context

Current framing:
- uses processing as one useful lens;
- prioritizes in natura/minimally processed foods and culinary preparations;
- avoids moralizing an isolated food.

Reviewer:
- [ ] aligned with Guia Alimentar;
- [ ] revise.

## Hydration

Current text explicitly says fluid needs vary with climate, sweat, activity, food, body size and individual context.

It does **not** give a universal litres/day number.

Reviewer should confirm:
- [ ] wording is appropriate;
- [ ] electrolyte framing is sufficiently cautious;
- [ ] clinical fluid restrictions are clearly outside scope.

## Pre/post workout

Current approach:
- comfort and whole-day intake before timing obsession;
- no “anabolic window closes in minutes” claim;
- higher-volume sport can require more specific strategy.

Reviewer:
- [ ] acceptable
- [ ] revise.

## Weight-management framing

The ebook rejects:
- detox claims;
- “fat-burning foods”;
- “accelerate metabolism” marketing;
- extreme compensation after eating out.

Reviewer:
- [ ] acceptable
- [ ] revise.

## Supplements / creatine — explicit high-attention review

These sections need direct professional confirmation because readers can easily interpret education as product advice.

Reviewer must check:
- [ ] no individualized dosing is prescribed;
- [ ] no supplement is presented as medically necessary;
- [ ] contamination/quality/interactions are acknowledged;
- [ ] therapeutic or stimulant-heavy products are outside generic scope;
- [ ] creatine evidence is described without implying universal need;
- [ ] disease/pregnancy/medication contexts redirect to an appropriate professional.

## Seven-day “reset”

This is explicitly framed as a **routine reset, not detox**:
- water/routine;
- shopping;
- meal bases;
- plant-food variety;
- difficult-day planning;
- cooking;
- reflection.

Reviewer:
- [ ] title/framing cannot reasonably be mistaken for physiological detox;
- [ ] acceptable;
- [ ] revise.

## Final nutrition-review checklist

- [ ] No individualized clinical diet prescription.
- [ ] No diagnosis/treatment claims.
- [ ] No universal calorie target.
- [ ] No universal protein gram target.
- [ ] No universal fluid target.
- [ ] Supplement/creatine sections sufficiently bounded.
- [ ] Eating-disorder context is not managed by generic advice.
- [ ] Pregnancy/medical/allergy complexities redirect appropriately.
- [ ] FoodData Central remains the source for numeric recipe nutrition.
- [ ] Approved version/date/reviewer role recorded.
