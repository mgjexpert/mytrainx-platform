## 1. BIA e balança inteligente: o que podem — e não podem — dizer

Slug:
`bia-balanca-inteligente-o-que-pode-e-nao-pode-dizer`

### Main claims to review

- consumer BIA measures impedance and estimates body-composition compartments through device algorithms;
- percentage body fat / fat-free mass are not direct tissue measurements;
- individual agreement with criterion methods can be poor even when group-level bias looks smaller;
- consistent device/conditions improve longitudinal interpretation but do not make estimates exact;
- image-based body-composition estimation remains inappropriate for false-precision clinical claims.

### Key evidence

- Oliver CJ et al. *The Validity of Bioelectrical Impedance Analysis Compared to a Four-Compartment Model in Healthy Adults: A Systematic Review.* 2026. PMID 41718193.
  - 12 studies;
  - percentage-fat limits of agreement commonly very wide;
  - BIA estimates were overall not equivalent to the 4C model at individual level.
- Poltronieri TS et al. *Mobile applications for body composition estimation: functionality, current findings, and future directions.* 2026. PMID 42335590.
  - promising technology;
  - reduced individual accuracy in some populations;
  - limited diverse-population and longitudinal validation.

### Reviewer questions

- Is the wording sufficiently clear that the same-device trend can still contain measurement error?
- Is “massa muscular” distinguished from fat-free/lean mass where relevant?
- Should any device-specific caveats be added?

## 2. Como medir a cintura de forma consistente

Slug:
`como-medir-cintura-de-forma-consistente`

### Main claims to review

- protocols can use different anatomical landmarks;
- serial comparison should keep the same protocol;
- WHO reference protocol uses the midpoint between the last palpable rib and top of iliac crest, tape horizontal and non-compressive, reading after normal expiration.

### Key evidence

WHO. *Waist circumference and waist–hip ratio: report of a WHO expert consultation.*
https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241501491

### Reviewer questions

- Is the anatomical instruction understandable enough for a lay user without increasing measurement error?
- Should waist measurement be framed only as longitudinal tracking in the product, avoiding automatic risk interpretation?

## 3. Como se pesar de forma consistente

Slug:
`como-se-pesar-de-forma-consistente`

### Main claims to review

- use the same scale and comparable conditions;
- an isolated reading may reflect short-term variation;
- trend views can reduce overreaction to single measurements;
- weighing frequency should remain optional.

### Evidence / limitations

- Schneditz D et al. *Day-to-day variability in euvolemic body mass.* 2023. PMID 37955103.
  - useful demonstration that standardized body mass varies;
  - **major limitation:** long-term dataset from one healthy male individual — do not generalize an exact expected percentage to all users.
- Hall KD et al. work on free-living body-weight dynamics describes short-term fluid effects of dietary sodium/carbohydrate and glycogen-related water.
  - use only for mechanism context, not individualized prediction.

### Reviewer questions

- Is anxiety/compulsive-behaviour language appropriately bounded and non-diagnostic?
- Should the app offer an explicit “hide weight” path everywhere weight is displayed?

## 4. Por que seu peso muda de um dia para o outro

Slug:
`por-que-seu-peso-muda-de-um-dia-para-o-outro`

### Main claims to review

Short-term scale changes can reflect:
- body water;
- gastrointestinal content;
- stored carbohydrate/glycogen-associated water;
- measurement timing and conditions.

### Guardrail

Do **not** translate a short-term weight delta into a claimed fat gain/loss calculation.

### Evidence caveat

The existing day-to-day variability source is useful but narrow (single-person dataset). The specialist should decide whether an additional broader physiology reference is required for final retrieval approval.

### Main claims to review

- photographs are documentation, not direct measurement of body fat or muscle mass;
- lighting, lens/distance, posture, clothing and contraction can strongly change visual comparison;
- consistent setup improves comparability;
- private-by-default storage and explicit consent for AI use are product requirements.

### Product guardrail

Coach X must not infer:
- diagnosis;
- precise body-fat percentage;
- precise muscle mass;
- pathology from appearance.

## Approval checklist

For each item, reviewer should confirm:

- [ ] Sources are appropriate for the exact claim.
- [ ] No source is generalized beyond its study population/design.
- [ ] Consumer measurement limitations are explicit.
- [ ] No diagnostic implication.
- [ ] No medication or treatment advice.
- [ ] No precise body-composition inference from photographs.
- [ ] “Trend” is not presented as “truth”.
- [ ] Optional metrics remain optional.
- [ ] Escalation language is appropriate when symptoms/clinical concerns appear.
